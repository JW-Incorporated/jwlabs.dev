Website privacy notice

Effective October 14, 2026. This notice covers this website, jwlabs.ai, operated by JW Labs LLC, a California limited liability company.

It does not cover the 4a app, which has its own, much longer and much more exact policy: 4a's privacy policy. That document lists every key the app stores on a device, every field it transmits and every host a device contacts, and it is published from the same repository that builds the app so the two cannot drift. If your question is about the app, that is the page you want.

longlive is not covered either. It has its own privacy policy at longlivets.com.

The short version

This website is a set of static HTML files, plus one small application: the signup and subscription pages for The Daily Brief, under /dailybrief/. It sets no cookies, runs no JavaScript, and has no analytics — the Daily Brief pages included. There is nothing here to log you in to, and no third party embedded in any page. The only thing to fill in is the Daily Brief signup form, and the section about it below says exactly what happens to what you type.

Why that is checkable rather than just claimed

You do not have to take our word for it, and neither does a browser extension.

  • There is no <script> tag on any page of this site. Not a deferred one, not an inline one, not one from a content delivery network. The build script that generates these pages fails if a script tag appears in its output, and it fails again if any element on a page would fetch anything from another host. The Daily Brief pages under /dailybrief/ are not made by that build — a Cloudflare Worker generates them — and they hold the same line: no script, no cookie, and nothing from another origin. For those pages that is a statement about how the Worker is written, and you can check it in your browser.
  • Nothing on a page is loaded from another origin. No web fonts, no hosted icons, no tracking pixel, no embedded video. The only things a page pulls are one stylesheet and one favicon, both from this domain. Every external URL on this site is an ordinary link you have to choose to follow.
  • Therefore there is no consent banner, because there is nothing to consent to. A cookie notice on this site would be theatre.

The reason for that discipline is not privacy marketing: 4a ships a strict Content Security Policy that blocks remote scripts and fonts, and this site holds the same line so the two cannot drift into different rules. Privacy is the side effect, and we will take it.

What our hosting providers see

This site is served as static files by GitHub Pages, with Cloudflare in front of it terminating TLS. Cloudflare is not incidental here — it is the edge that answers https://jwlabs.ai, so it sees every request before GitHub does. The one exception is /dailybrief/: those pages are answered by a Cloudflare Worker — a small program running on Cloudflare — and never reach GitHub at all, so GitHub does not see those requests.

Like every web server on the internet, those two receive what your browser sends in order to answer the request: your IP address, your user agent, the page you asked for, the time, and a referrer if your browser sent one. They are our service providers, they process that data to deliver and secure the site, and their own privacy terms apply to their handling of it.

To be precise about our own access rather than flattering:

  • We have no per-visitor record and no way to build one. There is no analytics script, no cookie, no identifier and no log we retain or query. The one record we do keep is about Daily Brief subscribers, not visitors, and it is described in full below.
  • Cloudflare's dashboard does show the account aggregate traffic counts for this domain — requests, bandwidth, cached-versus-uncached, rough geography — the way it does for any zone. That is a count, not a profile, and it exists whether we look at it or not. We are telling you because "we collect nothing" would be a slightly better sentence and a slightly less true one.

If you email us

If you write to help@jwlabs.ai, we hold your message and your email address, because that is what having a mailbox means. We use it to answer you and to keep a record of the correspondence. We do not add you to a mailing list — the only list we keep is The Daily Brief's, and you are on it only if you sign up yourself — and we do not sell, rent or share it. Ask us to delete a thread and we will.

The Daily Brief

The Daily Brief is a free weekday morning news email. Signing up for it is the one thing on this website that collects personal information, so this section says exactly what, where it is kept, and for how long.

What we keep. When you sign up we store:

  • your email address, as you typed it and in a normalised form so the same address cannot sign up twice;
  • the city you typed, and the place it resolved to — its name, region, country, coordinates and time zone — which decides the local news and weather in your copy;
  • a one-way hash of your IP address, mixed with a value that changes every day so it cannot be reversed into the address or matched across days. It is used only to limit how many signups can come from one place, which is how we keep the form from being abused;
  • the status of your subscription (waiting for confirmation, or active) and when each of those happened;
  • a hash of the private link in the footer of every issue, so that link can find your subscription without the link itself being stored;
  • a send record for each issue: the date, whether it was sent, and the delivery service's message reference or error.

Nothing else. No name, no age, no tracking pixel in the email, no record of whether or when you open it, and no record of which links you click.

Where it is kept. In a Cloudflare D1 database that we operate, and — your email address only — in the destination address list of Cloudflare Email Routing, the service that delivers the email. Cloudflare is a US-based provider acting for us, and its own privacy terms apply to its handling of that data.

Who sends the confirmation. Cloudflare only delivers our mail to an address that has confirmed it wants it, so when you sign up Cloudflare sends you a verification email, not us. Your subscription starts when you click Verify in it; until then nothing else is sent to you.

Who else sees what. To turn the city you typed into a place, that text — not your email address — is looked up with Open-Meteo's geocoding service; for a place in the United States its coordinates are also looked up with the National Weather Service. Each issue's local news and weather are prepared per place, not per person, from a list of places that contains no email addresses. Your email address goes only to Cloudflare, to deliver the email.

How long.

  • A signup that is never verified is deleted after 7 days, from our database and from Cloudflare's list.
  • When you unsubscribe, your address is removed from Cloudflare's list and your record, including its send records, is deleted.
  • Otherwise we keep your record for as long as you are subscribed.

No sharing. We do not sell, rent, share or disclose your email address or your place to anyone, and we use them for nothing except sending you The Daily Brief and answering mail you send us. The brief carries no advertising.

How to stop it, or have it deleted. Every issue has an unsubscribe link and a link to manage your subscription, where you can also change your city. Unsubscribing takes one click and deletes your record as described above. Or write to help@jwlabs.ai and we will do it for you.

What we do not do

  • No advertising, and no advertising network, on this site.
  • No selling or sharing of personal information. There is nothing to sell.
  • No behavioural profiling, no cross-site tracking, no fingerprinting.
  • No forms, except the Daily Brief signup at /dailybrief/, which is a small application served by Cloudflare rather than a static page. There is nowhere else on this site to type anything.
  • No accounts, and no passwords.

Children

This website is a company and product site, not a service aimed at children, and apart from a Daily Brief signup it collects nothing from anybody. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children.

What this document is, and is not

It was written by the people who built this site, and it is accurate about what this website does — the claims in "why that is checkable" are enforced by the build, not by good intentions.
