Barbecue: eight beats of a forty-beat history

A sample foray — one assembled sequence of stretches taken from real podcast episodes, played back to back.

Runs 21 minutes 56 seconds. Ten stretches, drawn from six episodes of six different shows.

Fire, satay, asado and jerk, then who cooked America's barbecue and who was credited for it.

What it is doing

There is a forty-beat outline of this subject. This sequence takes eight of those forty beats and no more, and says so in its own title.

That is the whole editorial decision. An earlier assembly of the same subject ran 61 minutes across 32 stretches and tried to cover everything the sources could support; it wandered into griddle cookery and eighteenth-century tavern dinners and stopped being about barbecue. The story of why it was cut is on the index page. Twenty-two minutes that stay on topic beat sixty-one that do not.

The episodes it is drawn from

Six episodes, six shows, named in full because attribution is the point:

  • Origin Stories — Episode 09: Did Cooking Make Us Human? (Re-release)
  • Satay? Okay! — 1. Satay and the Myth of Malaysian Cuisine
  • BBQ Radio Network — ARGENTINA OPEN FIRE COOKING with AL FRUGONI
  • The Moreish Podcast — The History of Jerk in Jamaica with Alyssa Sperry Bertrand
  • The Grill Coach — Adrian Miller and The History of BBQ
  • The BBQ Central Show — Meathead Says NO to Cast Iron; Robert Moss Holds BBQ School For July 2024

Note the last one. The stretches used from it are about the history and spread of American barbecue; the episode it sits in is a general-interest show whose headline that week was a disagreement about cast iron. That is the case for this whole idea — the twenty minutes you wanted was inside an episode you would never have opened, under a title that gave no clue it was there.
