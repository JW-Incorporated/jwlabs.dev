# 4a

4a is a daily podcast picker. Every day it gives you **four topic queues** — not
one endless feed — and it makes one of them deliberately unlike the other three,
so the thing you did not know to ask for still reaches you.

It runs in a browser right now, with no signup and no password:
**[open 4a](https://jw-incorporated.github.io/foray/)**.

## The problem it is for

You have eleven podcasts you like and about forty minutes. The apps you already
have are very good at two things: playing a show you have already chosen, and
showing you a list of the most popular shows in a category. Neither of those is
the question you actually have, which is *what should I listen to right now.*

The usual answer to that question is a feed. A feed hands the work back to you:
scroll, read, judge, scroll again, give up, replay something you have heard. 4a
takes a position instead. It picks four things, tells you why it picked each one,
and gets out of the way.

## What you see when you open it

Four cards. Each card is a **queue on one subject**, not a single episode, and
each one tells you what it is before you tap:

- **How many episodes are in it, and how long they run in total** — so you can
  match a queue to the time you actually have.
- **Which shows it draws from**, named. If it is all from one show, it says so. If
  it is drawn from four, it says which two and how many more.
- **What it starts with** — the title of the first episode, quoted, so the card is
  never a mystery box.

Three of the four are subjects you have shown an interest in. **The fourth is
badged `Stretch`, and it is deliberately something else.** Tap a card and you get
the queue.

There is a refresh control if none of the four appeal. There is no infinite
scroll, no "for you" river, and nothing that gets longer the more you look at it.

## The Stretch queue

This is the part of 4a that a metrics-driven product would delete.

The fourth queue is drawn from *next to* your demonstrated interests rather than
inside them. It carries a visible badge that says, in as many words, **"Outside
your usual topics, on purpose"** — because the alternative is a recommender
quietly sneaking something past you and hoping.

Two rules make it real rather than decorative:

**It does not back off when you skip it.** A slot that shrinks whenever it is
ignored is just a slower route back to the same three shows you already listen to.
Exploration that gives up is not exploration.

**It has to say what the bridge is** — the reason this is next to that, in a
single short line. A recommendation that cannot explain itself in eighteen words
has not earned the slot.

The honest cost: some Stretch picks will be wrong for you, and that is the design
working rather than failing. What we ask in return is that you tell it — see
[how it learns](/4a/features/#telling-it-when-it-is-wrong).

## Ask for a queue in your own words

Underneath the four cards there is one text field: **build me a playlist.**

Type what you actually want — a subject, a mood, a constraint, a mix — and 4a
interprets it against the library and assembles a queue. It relaxes the query if
it has to, and it tells you which of three things happened:

- It found a good queue.
- It found a **thin** one, and says so rather than padding it out with filler.
- It found nothing, in which case it **suggests adjacent subjects** that do have
  something in them, instead of shrugging.

Queues you build are saved as playlists, and the five most recent are one tap away
in the menu.

## It plays into whichever app you already use

By default, tapping an episode hands it off to the podcast app you already have —
Apple Podcasts, or a show page in another player — and you can change which one in
the settings. Your subscriptions, your queue, your history: 4a is not trying to
replace any of that.

4a also has its own player, used for assembled runs of segments. Either way,
**audio comes straight from the publisher's own servers.** 4a never rehosts,
proxies or transforms podcast audio, and there is no 4a server sitting between you
and an episode.

That has a consequence we would rather state on the way in than bury: because the
request goes directly to the publisher's host, that host — and any measurement
service the publisher has put in front of their own audio — sees your IP address
and browser, and 4a never does.
[What 4a knows about you](/4a/your-data/) is the plain-language version, and
[the privacy policy](/4a/privacy/) is the exact one.

## Where it runs

**A web app, today.** No signup, no password, no email address. It works on a
phone, it can be added to your home screen and behaves like an app once it is
there, and the app itself is cached — so it opens and renders even with no
connection. (Audio is never cached, so *playing* something needs a connection.)

**Native iOS and Android apps** are built from the same code as the web app, and
store releases are being prepared.
[What is built and what is not](/status/) is the honest inventory.

## Two words, and which is which

**4a** is the app.

A **foray** is a thing the app makes: one assembled run of segments, cut from
several episodes and played as a single sequence, with controls to jump to the
next or previous segment. The player is written and works, but **no foray is
published yet**, so it is not something you can open today. We would rather say
that here than let the word sit on this page as though it were a feature waiting
for you.

The app used to be called Foray, which is why the word still appears in the web
app's address and in the names of its on-device storage. Renaming those would
break saved links and orphan data already on people's devices, so they keep the
old name on purpose.

## What 4a will not do

These are not aspirations. Several of them are the reason other things are
harder.

**No engagement dark patterns.** No streaks, no infinite scroll, no autoplay
chains, no notification bait. Each of those raises time-in-app at the cost of the
thing the app is for.

**No advertising, and no ad tracking.** There is no ad code, no ad identifier and
no advertising SDK in 4a. There is also no analytics library and no crash
reporter, and no third-party SDK of any kind.

**No account, unless you want one.** No signup, no password, no email, no profile.

**We do not skip or strip advertising in anyone else's audio.** Not as a courtesy
— as a commitment that shapes the whole product. Playback is a seek and a stop
against the publisher's own file.

**We do not ask you to describe yourself.** There is no questionnaire to fill in
before the app works. It watches what you open and what you skip.
