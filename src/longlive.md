# longlive

longlive is a time machine through Taylor Swift's eras — a scrubbable timeline
where every moment is sourced and dated, back through all twelve of them.

It lives on its own domain: **[longlivets.com](https://longlivets.com/)**.

## What you do with it

**You drag through time and the moments come with you.** The timeline is a
scrubber: pull it and an era unspools underneath, moment by moment, each one dated
and attributed. Above it sits a ridge that bulges where the most happened, so a
decade of a career has a shape you can see before you have read a word of it.

The site's own instruction is the clearest description of the interaction there
is: *drag to scrub through the era. The ridge bulges where the most happened.*

## Filters, for the thing you actually came for

Nobody arrives wanting *everything*. The timeline filters down to the strand you
care about:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Music** | Releases, songs, the recorded work |
| **Videos** | The visual output |
| **Fashion** | What was worn, when, and where |
| **Tour** | Dates, shows, the road |
| **Relationship** | The documented, and the widely-discussed |
| **Lore** | The rest of the story a fan actually cares about |

Or **All**, which is the default and the one to scrub through when you do not know
what you are looking for yet.

## Threads

The timeline is chronological. A **thread** is the other axis: *one storyline at a
time, across every era.*

Pick a thread and the site pulls the moments belonging to that story out of ten
years of chronology and lays them end to end — so a narrative that was scattered
across four eras reads as a single line. Each has a follow control to stay with it.

This is the feature that answers the question a timeline cannot: not *what
happened in 2019*, but *how did this particular thing develop.*

## Eggs

*The secrets she plants.* Fan-culture's word for a deliberately placed clue, and a
category the timeline treats as its own — because the interesting thing about an
egg is not only what it was, but when it was planted and what it later turned out
to point at.

## Every moment is sourced and dated

This is the discipline the whole project is built on, and it is the same one behind
[4a](/4a/): **nothing is asserted more confidently than its source supports.**

Entries carry their date and their attribution. And where a story is a
widely-discussed **fan interpretation** rather than a confirmed fact, the timeline
says so **on the moment itself** — marked *unconfirmed*, right there, rather than in
a disclaimer at the bottom of the page that nobody reads.

That distinction is the entire reason the project is worth building. A timeline
that quietly mixes reporting with theory is a rumour with dates on it. One that
marks the difference lets you enjoy the theory *as* a theory, which is what a fan
wanted in the first place.

## What a moment actually looks like

Rather than describe the format, here are two real entries from the timeline, in
the shape the site shows them.

---

**October 3, 2025** · *Career-defining* · **Music**

> **The Life of a Showgirl arrives: 12 tracks, Max Martin and Shellback, one
> guest.** Written and recorded in Sweden between European Eras Tour dates — her
> first full album with Max Martin and Shellback since *1989* and *reputation*.

---

**August 24, 2026** · **Relationship**

> **Adam Scott on the wedding: "at its core, it just felt like a regular
> wedding".** The actor, a guest at the July 3 wedding, told *TODAY* that for all
> the Madison Square Garden scale — "more people than were at my wedding" — the
> night felt ordinary, and that everyone teared up.

---

Four things are doing work in each of those, and they are the whole format:

- **A date**, precise to the day, which is what makes the timeline scrubbable at
  all.
- **A category** — the filters above — so the same era reads differently depending
  on what you came for.
- **A short line that says what happened**, written to be read at scrubbing speed
  rather than to be comprehensive.
- **A second line with the substance and the attribution**: who reported it, and
  where. The first entry names the collaborators and the recording circumstances;
  the second names the outlet.

And where an entry is a **theory rather than a fact**, it is marked **Unconfirmed**,
on the moment, in the same place your eye already is. That is the one piece of
formatting the whole project rests on: a reader scrubbing quickly should never have
to wonder which of these two things they are looking at.

## What it is not

**longlive is an independent, fan-made project.** It is **not affiliated with,
endorsed by, or connected to Taylor Swift or her representatives.** The site says
so itself, in as many words, and adds the necessary companion: all narratives are
widely-discussed fan interpretations, not confirmed fact.

It was previously called swift2, which is why that name turns up in older
references.
