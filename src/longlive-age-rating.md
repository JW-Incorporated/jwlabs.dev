# longlive — content and age suitability

[longlive](/longlive/) is a timeline through Taylor Swift's eras: dated, sourced
moments you scrub through. This page says what is in it and who it suits.

**The short version:** longlive is a read-only reference timeline. **Its content is
suitable for a general audience, and it is not directed at children** — it is made
for fans, who are mostly teenagers and adults. There are no ads, no purchases, no
chat, and nothing any other user can post.

**It is an independent, fan-made project.** It is **not affiliated with, endorsed
by, or connected to Taylor Swift or her representatives.**

## What is in it

Dated entries about a public figure's public career, grouped into six strands:
**music, videos, fashion, tour, relationship** and **lore**. Each carries a date, a
short line saying what happened, and an attribution to whoever reported it.

Where an entry is a widely-discussed fan interpretation rather than a confirmed
fact, it is **marked unconfirmed on the entry itself** — not in a disclaimer at the
bottom that nobody reads. That distinction is the discipline the whole project runs
on.

The **relationship** strand covers what has been publicly reported and publicly
discussed: who was together, when, and what was said about it afterwards. It is
career and press reporting, at the level a newspaper's culture desk would carry.

**What is not there:** no lyrics, in any quantity, ever. No album art. No song or
video files. No violence, no sexual content, no profanity, no gambling, no drug or
alcohol themes presented as such.

## Who we think it is for

**Fans, which in practice means teenagers and adults.** Nothing in the timeline is
unsuitable for a younger reader, but the app assumes you already care about the
subject and can read at length — and we do not market it to children or design it
for them.

We do not ask anyone's age.

## Where a store's rating comes from

Each app store sets its own rating from its own questionnaire, and the result shows
on that store's listing. Those ratings are the stores' classifications, not ours,
and the scales differ between them. This page is the description behind them.

One thing worth separating, because it looks like a contradiction and is not: a
rating describes **content**, while a store's "target audience" describes **who an
app is aimed at**. Content suitable for anyone is not the same as an app built for
children, and longlive is the first without being the second.
