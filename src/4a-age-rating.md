# 4a — content and age suitability

[4a](/4a/) is a daily podcast picker. This page says what is actually in it, who it
suits, and what we do and do not control — so that a parent, a listener, or a store
reviewer can decide for themselves rather than take a single number on trust.

**The short version:** 4a is a general-audience app. **It is not directed at
children, and we recommend it for listeners aged 13 and over.** It carries no ads,
no purchases, no chat and no user-generated content. The podcasts inside it are made
by independent publishers, and what their hosts say is not under our control.

## What is in the app

Two kinds of content, and the distinction matters for everything below.

**Podcast episodes from independent publishers.** This is almost all of it. 4a
holds a library of shows — history, science, engineering, business, food, comedy and
more — and picks stretches of episodes for you. We did not make this material, we do
not host it, and we do not edit it. It plays from the publisher's own file, exactly
as they published it.

**Our own written introductions.** Short lines written by us that say why something
was picked and what to expect. These are ours, and we hold them to the standard on
this page.

## What we control, and what we do not

**We choose what to offer you.** Which shows are in the library, which episode, and
which stretch of it. That is the editorial act, and it is the one we are
accountable for.

**We do not control what a host says.** A history episode can turn to atrocity. A
business interview can swear. A comedy show is a comedy show. Podcasts are made for
adults by default, and the interesting ones do not sanitise themselves.

**We never alter the audio.** No re-cutting, no bleeping, no removal. An episode
plays as its publisher made it. That is a deliberate rule about respecting
publishers' work, and one of its consequences is that we cannot clean up an episode
even if we wanted to.

## There is no content filter

**4a does not filter its library by content, and it has no parental controls.**
Everything in the library is available to anyone using the app.

That is worth saying plainly, because the machinery to do it well does not exist.
Podcast feeds carry an "explicit" flag their publisher can set, but most never set
it: of the 220 shows in the library, **7 declare one; the rest say nothing either
way** *(measured, 2026-09-01)*. A filter built on a signal that is usually absent
would catch a little and imply it caught everything, which is worse than not
offering one.

So the honest description is the one at the top of this page: 4a is a
general-audience app for listeners 13 and over, and an adult deciding whether it
suits a younger listener should read this page rather than trust a toggle.

## What is not in the app

Several of the things that usually drive an age rating up are simply absent:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Advertising** | None. We show no ads and run no ad network. |
| **Purchases** | None. There is nothing to buy inside the app. |
| **User-generated content** | None. Nobody can post anything that another listener sees. |
| **Social features** | None. No profiles, no following, no comments, no messaging. |
| **Chat or AI conversation** | None. |
| **Open web browsing** | None. There is no in-app browser to wander out of. |
| **Accounts** | Not required. You can use 4a without signing up for anything. |
| **Location** | Never requested. |
| **Gambling, simulated gambling, contests** | None. |

Episodes may of course carry the advertising their own publishers sold. We do not
place it, we do not benefit from it, and we do not remove it.

## Who we think it is for

**Teenagers and adults.** The library is built around curiosity — how things work,
how they came to be, why an argument is unsettled — and the reading level and
attention span it assumes are an older teenager's and up.

**It is not a children's app**, we do not market it to children, and we do not ask
anyone's age. That last point is deliberate: asking would mean collecting it. See
[what 4a knows about you](/4a/your-data/).

## Where a store's rating comes from

Each app store sets its own rating from its own questionnaire, and the result
appears on that store's listing for the app. Those ratings are the stores'
classifications, not ours, and they use different scales that do not translate
cleanly into one another. **This page is the description behind them** — what is in
the app, in our own words, at more length than a rating badge can carry.
