# Barbecue: eight beats of a forty-beat history

A sample [foray](/4a/sample/) — one assembled sequence of stretches taken from real
podcast episodes, played back to back.

**Runs 21 minutes 56 seconds. Ten stretches, drawn from six episodes of six
different shows.**

> Fire, satay, asado and jerk, then who cooked America's barbecue and who was
> credited for it.

## What it is doing

There is a forty-beat outline of this subject. This sequence takes **eight of those
forty beats** and no more, and says so in its own title.

That is the whole editorial decision. An earlier assembly of the same subject ran
61 minutes across 32 stretches and tried to cover everything the sources could
support; it wandered into griddle cookery and eighteenth-century tavern dinners and
stopped being about barbecue.
[The story of why it was cut](/4a/sample/#the-one-we-cut) is on the index
page. Twenty-two minutes that stay on topic beat sixty-one that do not.

## The sequence

Descriptions are ours. There is no transcript text on this page.

### 1. Fire, smoke and the mechanism

| | Runs | From |
|---|---|---|
| Cooking selected for smaller teeth and guts; chimps chew up to six hours daily, we chew one. | 2:33 | *Origin Stories* |
| Controlled fire is hard to prove; Wonderwerk Cave pushes it back a million years. | 1:26 | *Origin Stories* |

Two stretches, four minutes, and the subject is established as a biological one
before it is a culinary one.

### 2. The world's parallel answers

| | Runs | From |
|---|---|---|
| Three rival origin theories for satay, then the point that skewered meat is universal. | 1:20 | *Satay? Okay!* |
| Asado is not a meal but a way of living — a four-to-six-hour party, every occasion. | 2:29 | *BBQ Radio Network* |
| Argentina's grill has a firebox at the side; you cook once the embers glow red. | 1:41 | *BBQ Radio Network* |
| Jerk began as preservation — wood ash instead of salt, a pit, and pimento wood smoke. *(a conversational exchange rather than one voice explaining)* | 3:58 | *The Moreish Podcast* |

The load-bearing section. Three continents answering the same question
independently, which is the argument against treating barbecue as an American
invention — made by putting three shows that have never met next to each other.

### 3. The American case

| | Runs | From |
|---|---|---|
| Adrian Miller names the tasks, and says plainly why enslaved African Americans were made to do them. *(an exchange)* | 2:51 | *The Grill Coach* |
| Miller's sources are old newspapers, and he dates when they began naming the cook. | 1:30 | *The Grill Coach* |
| American barbecue took root in Virginia, then rode west with the settlers to gold-rush California. | 2:31 | *The BBQ Central Show* |

Two stretches with the historian Adrian Miller on who actually did this work and
when the record began crediting them, then Robert Moss on where it spread. The
second Miller stretch is about his *sources* — which is what makes the first one
something other than an assertion.

### 4. The re-pricing

| | Runs | From |
|---|---|---|
| Barbecue faded after the war, revived in the 1990s; the German mustard story is doubtful. | 1:38 | *The BBQ Central Show* |

One stretch to close, and it includes a debunking. A sequence that ends by
undermining a popular origin story is a better ending than one that ends on a
flourish.

## The episodes it is drawn from

Six episodes, six shows, named in full because attribution is the point:

- **Origin Stories** — *Episode 09: Did Cooking Make Us Human? (Re-release)*
- **Satay? Okay!** — *1. Satay and the Myth of Malaysian Cuisine*
- **BBQ Radio Network** — *ARGENTINA OPEN FIRE COOKING with AL FRUGONI*
- **The Moreish Podcast** — *The History of Jerk in Jamaica with Alyssa Sperry
  Bertrand*
- **The Grill Coach** — *Adrian Miller and The History of BBQ*
- **The BBQ Central Show** — *Meathead Says NO to Cast Iron; Robert Moss Holds BBQ
  School For July 2024*

Note the last one. The stretches used from it are about the history and spread of
American barbecue; the episode it sits in is a general-interest show whose headline
that week was a disagreement about cast iron. **That is the case for this whole
idea** — the twenty minutes you wanted was inside an episode you would never have
opened, under a title that gave no clue it was there.
