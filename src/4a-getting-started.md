# Getting started with 4a

There is nothing to install, no account to make and nothing to configure before it
works. This page is here for the parts that are not obvious, and for what the
first week actually feels like.

**[Open 4a](https://jw-incorporated.github.io/foray/)** — it is a web address. If
you are on your phone, that is already the whole setup.

## The first thirty seconds

You will see four cards. A short introduction sits above them explaining what they
are; you can dismiss it and it stays dismissed.

Tap a card and you get its queue: the episodes in it, in order, with a total
running time. Tap an episode and it opens in whichever podcast app you already
use. That is the loop.

One of the four cards carries a **`Stretch`** badge. That one is deliberately not
about a subject you have shown an interest in — see
[why it exists](/4a/#the-stretch-queue) — and if it looks like a mistake on day
one, it is not. It is the point.

## Put it on your home screen

4a is a web app that installs. Once it is on your home screen it gets its own
icon and its own window, with no browser furniture, and it starts the way an app
starts.

**On iPhone or iPad:** open 4a in Safari, tap the **Share** button, then **Add to
Home Screen**.

**On Android:** open 4a in Chrome, open the browser menu, then **Install app** or
**Add to Home screen**, depending on your version.

**On a desktop:** most browsers show an install control in the address bar. It
works, and it is genuinely more pleasant than a tab.

You do not have to do any of this. Everything works in a normal tab.

## The two settings worth knowing about

Both are in the menu behind the **☰** button.

**Open in.** Which podcast app an episode hands off to. Change it once and forget
it.

**Family mode.** Hides episodes rated explicit, and also hides the whole comedy
category — because older comedy items in the library predate per-episode ratings
and we would rather over-hide than let one through. It is a blunt rule and we say
so; [the features page](/4a/features/#family-mode) explains why blunt is the right
shape here.

## What the first week looks like

Honest version, because the alternative is you concluding it is broken.

**Day one** the picks are built from very little. 4a does not ask you to describe
yourself — there is no questionnaire, no "choose five interests" screen — so on the
first day it is working from almost nothing and the queues will be broad.

**By the end of the first week** it has watched what you opened, what you skipped,
what you saved and what you finished, and the three non-Stretch queues start
looking like yours. What moves the needle fastest is *opening things* — including
opening something and abandoning it, which is a real signal and not a failure.

**The Stretch queue never converges**, by design. If it ever starts feeling
comfortable, something has gone wrong.

**If you want to skip the wait**, type what you want into the *build me a playlist*
field. That works from the first minute and does not need any history.

## If you want to shortcut the learning

Three things tell 4a more, faster:

- **Save things** with the star, even ones you do not play immediately.
- **Mark something done** with the ✓ on the Continue strip when you finish it.
- **Use `more like` and `less like`** where you already know the answer instead of
  waiting to demonstrate it.

## Offline

The app and its library listing are cached, so 4a **opens and renders with no
connection.** Audio is never cached, so **playing** something needs one. Worth
knowing before a flight.

## Things that will look like bugs and are not

- **The same show appearing in two queues.** Queues are subjects, and a show can
  legitimately be about two subjects.
- **A queue with one episode in it.** The card says how many; a queue of one is a
  subject where there was one good thing rather than four mediocre ones.
- **A playlist flagged as thin.** 4a found less than it wanted and told you,
  instead of padding it with filler.
- **An empty result offering different subjects.** That is the intended behaviour
  for a query the library cannot answer.
- **Nothing to resume.** The Continue strip only appears for something long enough
  that resuming is the point, and it expires rather than following you around.

## It is early software

4a is new, the library changes, and features move. If something reads as broken it
may well be, and one email address reaches a person who can fix it:
[{{MAIL}}](mailto:{{MAIL}}).

[The support page](/4a/support/) says what to include so a report can actually be
acted on — in particular, for anything to do with audio, the built-in
**Playback diagnostics** record, which lives on your device and is never sent
anywhere unless you copy it into an email yourself.
