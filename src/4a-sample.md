# What a foray looks like

The hardest thing to explain about [4a](/4a/) is the thing it is building towards.
So rather than describe it again, here are three real ones, in full.

A **foray** is a named sequence on one subject, assembled out of stretches taken
from real episodes of real podcasts and played back to back as a single run. Not a
playlist of whole episodes — the individual stretches, in an order somebody chose,
so that forty minutes covers a subject instead of covering one guest's whole
afternoon.

## The three

| | Runs | Stretches | Drawn from |
|---|---|---|---|
| **[Barbecue: eight beats of a forty-beat history](/4a/sample/barbecue/)** | 21:56 | 10 | 6 episodes of 6 different shows |
| **[The types of capital a startup can raise](/4a/sample/startup-capital/)** | 51:22 | 22 | 8 episodes of 7 different shows |
| **[How Earth got plate tectonics and Venus never did](/4a/sample/plate-tectonics/)** | 40:20 | 19 | 11 episodes of one show |

Those three shapes are deliberate. The first is a wide sweep across six unrelated
shows that have never been in a room together. The second is a reference document
on a practical question, assembled from seven. The third is one excellent show's
back catalogue, re-cut into the single explanation its episodes add up to but never
quite deliver.

## Read one of them and the product is obvious

That is the claim, and it is why these pages exist. Each one lists every stretch in
order, what it covers, how long it runs, and which episode of which show it came
from.

Look at [the geology one](/4a/sample/plate-tectonics/) in particular. It is eleven
separate interviews with eleven different scientists, cut down to nineteen stretches
and forty minutes, and the result answers a question no single episode asked. That
is the thing 4a is for.

## Where these actually stand

**All four assembled forays are drafts. None is published in the app**, so this is
not a page about something you can open today.

What is real: the sequences below are assembled, the source episodes are verified,
and the player that runs them is written and works — it has speed control and
next/previous-segment navigation. What is missing is publication. We would rather
show you the content and tell you its status than let the word "foray" sit on
[the product page](/4a/) as though it were a feature waiting for you.

[What is built and what is not](/status/) keeps that kind of thing in one list.

## The one we threw away

There were four. The first, an earlier assembly of the barbecue subject, ran **61
minutes across 32 stretches** and was superseded by the 22-minute version you can
read here.

It was cut for a reason worth publishing: it wandered. A foray about the history of
cooking over fire spent long stretches on griddle and bakestone cookery, Welsh
cakes and eighteenth-century tavern dinners — all genuinely interesting, none of it
the promised subject. The founder's verdict on reading it was that it had drifted
off plot, and he was right.

So the replacement covers **eight beats of a forty-beat history rather than
everything the sources could support**, and it names that constraint in its own
title. Three times shorter, and it is about barbecue.

We keep the long one rather than deleting it, because "here is the version that was
too long and here is why" is more useful to us than a clean archive. And an
assembled run that is *nearly* on topic is the characteristic failure of this whole
idea, so it is worth having one on file.

## What the descriptions are, and are not

Every line describing a stretch on these pages is **our own writing** — an
editorial note about what that stretch covers, written by us, capped at roughly a
line. There is no transcript text on these pages and there are no quotations from
anybody's episode.

Where a stretch is a conversational exchange rather than one person explaining
something, the page says so, because the two are different listening experiences.

**Naming shows and episodes is attribution, not affiliation.** Every podcast named
on these pages is an independent show with no connection to {{ORG}}. They have not
endorsed 4a, are not partners, and in most cases do not know we exist. We name them
because naming your sources is the correct thing to do, and because
[4a never rehosts, transforms or re-serves anybody's audio](/4a/for-podcasters/) —
playback is always from the publisher's own file on the publisher's own server.

If you host one of these shows and would rather not be named here or included at
all, [{{MAIL}}](mailto:{{MAIL}}) and the answer is yes.
